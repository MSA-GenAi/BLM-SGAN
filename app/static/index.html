      Graduation project

      BLM-SGAN

      Bidirectional Language-Modeling for Semantic Spatial Text-to-Image Generation. A pretrained bidirectional language model (BERT) encodes the description. A single-stage GAN generator then turns noise into a 256×256 image, using semantic masks to decide where the text should change the image.

      Pipeline

      InputCaption “a red bird with black wings”
      TextBERT-base, uncased 12 layers, top 2 fine-tuned · up to 40 word pieces
      Sentencemean of token states → 256 projection trained with DAMSM
      GeneratorNetG · 7 G-Blocks 4×4 → 256×256, mask-guided affine
      Output256×256 image + 7 semantic masks

      Semantic-spatial conditioning

      Noise z ∈ ℝ¹⁰⁰ is mapped to a 4×4×512 feature map. Each G-Block upsamples it 2× and applies an affine transform whose scale and shift are predicted from the sentence embedding. A predicted mask m ∈ [0,1] gates that transform per pixel: γ·m + 1 and β·m. This is designed to let the text act on the bird more than on the background. The mask strip in each result shows m.

      Training setup

      Dataset
      CUB-200-2011 birds: 8,855 training images, 10 captions each
      Text encoder
      BERT-DAMSM, pre-trained for 40 epochs, then frozen
      Discriminator
      NetD, 6 residual down-blocks, conditional logits
      Losses
      Hinge adversarial + mismatch, MA-GP (k = 2, p = 6), DAMSM × 0.05
      Optimiser
      Adam, G 1e-4 / D 4e-4, β = (0, 0.9)
      Batch / epochs
      24 / 600; epoch 555 is the reported model

      Text encoders

      Caption encoding

      The caption is lower-cased and split into words, like the training captions, and BERT’s WordPiece tokenizer reads it. Words that never occur in the CUB captions still get meaningful features, and captions up to 40 word pieces are read whole.

      Sentence embedding

      The 256-d sentence vector is the mean of BERT’s token states, passed through a projection trained together with a DAMSM image encoder. On real CUB test photos it ranks the right caption first among 100 in 16.3% of cases, on par with AttnGAN’s LSTM encoder (16.9%).

      SSA-GAN baseline

      SSA-GAN reads captions with AttnGAN’s DAMSM LSTM over the 5,450-word CUB vocabulary: unknown words are dropped and only the first 18 words are used. Each model gets the text encoder it was trained with.

      CUB test split, 2,933 images, one generated image per test image. Run evaluation/run_all.sh to fill this table.

      No evaluation results yet.

      Both FIDs compare Inception-v3 features of the 2,933 generated images with the real test images. SSA-GAN protocol uses the whole test photos, as SSA-GAN’s own evaluation does. On it, the released SSA-GAN scores 15.48 against the 15.61 its paper reports, so this column can be compared with the paper. Bird crops uses the test photos cropped to the bird, like the training images. IS uses StackGAN’s CUB-fine-tuned Inception-v3, the classifier CUB papers use (10 splits); real photos score 22.3. On the papers’ 30k-image protocol, SSA-GAN scores 5.10 ± 0.06 against its reported 5.17 ± 0.08. IS ignores the caption. CLIP score is the cosine similarity between an image and its caption (CLIP ViT-B/32); real photos score 0.284. R-precision is how often an image’s own caption ranks first among 100 captions. DAMSM is the standard CUB judge, but models trained with a DAMSM loss (like SSA-GAN) partly optimise for it. CLIP is an independent judge.

      No language tests yet (evaluation/language_tests.py).

      Rare words: colour and size words are replaced by synonyms that SSA-GAN’s training captions contain at most twice (red → vermilion, white → alabaster, large → sizable, …), and each image is scored against the original caption. “Kept” is the share of the caption’s effect that survives: (rare − random caption) / (original − random caption). Long captions: test captions longer than 18 words. SSA-GAN reads only the first 18 words; BLM-SGAN’s BERT reads the whole caption. Each image is scored against the words after the 18th; chance R-precision is 1%.
